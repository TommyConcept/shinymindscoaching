Advice for parents • Anxiety

Helping an anxious girl: 7 gentle tools for calm and confidence

Sunday night wobbles. Test mornings. Refusing the invitation to the party. If you're navigating life with an anxious girl, these are the simple tools I teach in sessions — and you can start them together tonight.

First, a re-frame that changes everything: anxiety in your daughter is not misbehaviour, and it's not “too much”. It's a nervous system doing its very best to protect her — just a little too eagerly. Once you see her worry that way, connection gets easier and so does calming it. Rule one with an anxious girl: calm the body first, reason second.

Seven tools to try tonight

1. Hot chocolate breathing

Before she drinks it, have her smell the hot chocolate deeply… then blow across the top to cool it down, slowly. Smell in, long breath out. It naturally slows her breathing and tells her body it's safe — disguised as a treat.

2. The enormous, squeezy hug

Deep pressure is calming and organising for many children. A long wrap-around hug, or a snug blanket cocoon, helps a buzzing body settle. Watch her shoulders; when they drop, her nervous system just exhaled.

3. Sway in the kitchen

Put on one slow song and sway together. Rhythmic, gentle movement is one of the fastest ways to settle a worried body — and it builds the feeling that you're a safe place to land.

4. Name the worry (like a scientist, not a judge)

“Where do you feel it? Is it big or small? Warm or prickly?” Naming a feeling with curiosity reduces its intensity. What we can point at, we don't have to fight in the dark.

5. Thoughts are not facts

When the “what ifs” come, teach her to ask: “Is that thought true? Is it helping?” An anxious girl often believes her thoughts fully; this question gently widens the gap between her and the worry.

6. The worry ladder

Avoiding fears teaches the brain they're dangerous. Instead, draw a ladder: at the bottom, the smallest brave step (a bit of the new club with you sitting in the car); at the top, the whole thing. Step by step wins.

7. Anchor her in who she is

Comparison is fuel for anxiety. Remind her whose she is: “You are fearfully and wonderfully made” (Psalm 139:14). Her worth isn't her last result, her spot in the reading group, or who copied her hairstyle first.

When to bring in a kid’s life coach

These tools help most families take the edge off. But if the wobbles pour into whole weeks, if she's frozen and closed off, or if you can't reach the girl you used to know, it's worth talking to a kid’s life coach. That's exactly the work I do at Shiny Minds: 1:1 coaching for girls aged 8-13 that's warm, gentle and shaped around her — and for Christian parents who want faith woven in.

And please do trust your instincts: if what you're seeing looks like more than everyday worry, speak to your GP. Coaching and clinical care are friends, not competitors.

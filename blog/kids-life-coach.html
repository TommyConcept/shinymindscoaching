Advice for parents • Kid’s life coaching

What does a kid’s life coach do?

A parent-friendly guide to how a kid’s life coach helps worries, confidence and big feelings — and how it's different from therapy.

If your daughter has been struggling with worry, shyness or self-doubt, you may have found yourself typing “kid’s life coach” into Google late at night, wondering exactly what that person does — and whether they can help your daughter. Here's what kid’s life coaching is, what happens in a session, and how to know if it's the right kind of support for her.

A kid’s life coach helps her manage the present — and rewires how she meets the future

A kid’s life coach works with the worries a child is facing now: the sleepover she's dreading, the test tomorrow, the voice in her head that says everyone else is better than me. Rather than diving into her past, coaching is forward-facing. It gives her practical, child-friendly tools for calming her body, questioning her worried thoughts and taking small brave steps — the same kinds of skills an adult might learn from a life coach, translated into a language an 8-13 year old understands.

At Shiny Minds, that means four things happen across coaching:

  • She learns to calm her body first. Slow breathing, deep-pressure and gentle movement settle her nervous system. A calm body is easier to re-think with.
  • She learns that thoughts are not facts. She names her “what ifs”, and practises testing them against reality — like a detective, gently.
  • She learns who she really is. Comparison loses its grip when she remembers her worth isn't her last result. (This is where the faith element of our coaching shines — more below.)
  • She takes brave steps, one at a time. With a plan she helped shape, school, clubs and new places become possible again.

Is a kid’s life coach the same as a therapist?

This is the question parents ask most, and it matters. Coaching is not therapy. Kid’s life coaching is educational and practical: it teaches skills for confidence, anxiety and everyday feelings. A therapist (or counsellor) diagnoses and treats mental health conditions. If your daughter needs clinical support, a good kid’s life coach will tell you straight away and point you to your GP — that's an important part of keeping children safe.

Many families use both: therapy treats the deeper wound; coaching builds the day-to-day confidence, courage and calm. If you are unsure which your daughter needs, that's exactly what a free consultation is for.

What does a kid’s life coaching session look like at Shiny Minds?

First comes the free 30-minute chat with you, the parent. You tell me about your daughter and what she is facing; I explain how coaching could help; there is no pressure and no obligation.

Sessions themselves are warm, playful and practical. Depending on her age and personality, a session might include games that teach brain science, drawing a “worry ladder” we'll climb one rung at a time, or practising the breathing tools she can use at home. Nothing is forced. Coaching moves at her pace, and parents are included at every stage with simple language and routines to use between sessions.

Faith, gently woven in

For Christian parents, it's a comfort to know the coaching is not spiritually neutral. Kid’s life coaching at Shiny Minds is rooted in Christian faith, offered gently and never force: science-approved calming tools, alongside the truth that she is fearfully and wonderfully made (Psalm 139:14) and that God is close when she feels crushed in spirit (Psalm 34:18).

“Be careful not to raise her with feet rooted in sand. Equip her with both: science-backed tools for her butterflies and scripture underneath every sense of who she is.”Mary Heather, Shiny Minds Coaching

Could a kid’s life coach help your daughter?

A kid’s life coach could help if your daughter is coping on the outside but quietly anxious inside, if she tummy-hurts before school every Sunday night, if she is comparing herself to everyone, or if perfectionism never switches off. If she is struggling to the point of needing clinical help, coaching is not the right first step — your GP is, and I'll happily help you think that through.

The first step is always free and entirely yours.

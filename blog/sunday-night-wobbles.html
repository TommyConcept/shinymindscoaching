Sunday night wobbles: a kid’s life coaching plan for the weekend before school

Around 6pm on Sunday it starts: tummy aches, quiet tears, dread on repeat. If school nerves turn your weekend upside down, here’s the plan I give families in kid’s life coaching — Friday to Monday morning.

Sunday night wobbles are one of the most common things parents mention to me. The weekend is safe; Monday is a mountain. The cycle is real: Friday relief → Saturday calm → Sunday evening dread. The good news is that the Sunday night window is also the best teaching moment of the week — your daughter is telling you exactly where her worry lives.

Friday: set the weekend up for brave

Don’t let the weekend disappear into screens and late nights. Keep consistent bedtimes and wake-ups, and plan one small social or brave moment early in the weekend — meeting a friend, a trip to the shop counter. Avoiding people all weekend teaches the worry that the world is dangerous; small brave moments keep the muscle warm.

Saturday: check the worry’s temperature together

Somewhere calm, ask: “What course do you feel like you’re on for Monday — green, amber or red?” No fixing, just listening. Then help her name the exact worry: not “school” but “nobody will play with me at break” or “I’ll get changed in front of everyone”. Specific worries can be planned for. Vague ones can’t.

Sunday evening: the gentle countdown

  • Warm dinner, warm body. A bath and a family meal lower physical arousal before the hardwood conversations.
  • Hot chocolate breathing. Smell in slowly, blow the steam away even slower. Five rounds.
  • The worry card. She writes or draws the worry, folds it, and you each carry part of it out of the room — the worry is literally handed over for the night.
  • Scripture anchor. For Christian parents: “Cast all your anxiety on Him because He cares for you” (1 Peter 5:7). Say it together, not as a lecture but as a blessing.
  • Picture Monday. Walk through the day step by step, from breakfast to hometime, landing on one thing she’s looking forward to.

Monday morning: keep it boring

Mornings go badly when they’re rushed. Up 15 minutes earlier, breakfast the same every day, outfit laid out Sunday, and a goodbye ritual that’s identical every day — the same hug, the same three words. Boring is safety. If she struggles at the gate, keep goodbyes short, warm and predictable, and let the teacher take over quickly; long lingering farewells feed the fear.

When Sunday wobbles mean more

If this has run for many weeks, if she’s missing full days, or the panic is rising instead of easing, it’s time for help beyond home tools. That’s where kid’s life coaching comes in: a neutral adult, a brave-step plan, calming tools she owns. Not sure whether it’s shyness or anxiety? Start with shy or anxious girl: how to tell the difference. And if the struggle looks bigger than coaching, your GP is the right first stop — and I’ll always say so plainly.
